# First Outreach — Target Checklist & Message Templates

_First version, written 2026-08-22. Scope: Shyama's personal network only — channel #1 of the priority list already set in `agents/gtm-lead.md`. This is deliberately not a volume-outreach plan; it's a filter for who to talk to first, and real templates for that first conversation. WhatsApp-community seeding, content, and accountant partnerships stay as later-phase channels per `agents/gtm-lead.md` — don't pull them forward here._

_Companion documents: `PITCH_DECK.md` (what to say once you're in the conversation), `DEMO_SCRIPT.md` (what to show live)._

---

## Why this is a conversation, not a pitch you send

Two things point the same direction here. First, the ICP hypothesis in `agents/gtm-lead.md` is explicitly unvalidated — "Trust signal: word-of-mouth in community, not cold email" is a guess, not a confirmed fact. Second, `Core Product & Vision/vision.md` carries a standing board condition: the calmer external positioning is only a placeholder until `Core Product & Vision/SURVEY.md` has actually been run against real owners. Those two facts point at the same move: **the first several conversations should do double duty** — pitch and validate at once — rather than optimising for a fast close. You don't need to run all 25 questions in `SURVEY.md` like a form. But Section 2 of it (trust and control — Q7–11) tests the single most important open assumption in the whole product: will an owner actually accept an AI sending anything to their customer without them seeing it first. Work one or two of those questions naturally into the first real conversation, before or after the pitch. If five different owners give you the same answer, that's worth more than the pitch closing.

## Target-profile checklist

Run every name through this before reaching out. Don't skip straight to "who's most likely to say yes" — the first client also has to be someone whose early experience (good or bad) travels well through the community, since referral is the second channel in the plan.

**Business fit — must have most of these:**
- [ ] Wholesale, distribution, import, or trading business — not retail-only, not services
- [ ] Genuinely WhatsApp-native — enquiries and orders actually happen there, not just casual chat
- [ ] Team small enough that a missed message is a real, felt problem (roughly 2–10 people) — a 50-person operation with a dedicated sales desk won't feel this pain the same way
- [ ] You've personally heard them complain, even once, about something this fixes — a missed order, a forgotten quote, chasing an invoice, "I don't know how I keep track of it all"

**Relationship fit — must have all of these:**
- [ ] They'd take your call or reply to your WhatsApp within a day, unprompted
- [ ] You could ask them a slightly awkward business question (how much do you turn over, what do you currently lose to this) and they'd actually answer
- [ ] They've said yes to trying something of yours before, or you're confident they would — a first "yes" is easier to get from someone who's already shown they'll try things for you

**Readiness fit — must have most of these:**
- [ ] Comfortable enough with a phone/laptop to log in and follow a QR-code setup without hand-holding beyond what you're already offering
- [ ] Not the most change-resistant person in their own business — if they'd need to convince a co-owner or a sceptical family member first, that's a slower close, note it but don't rule them out
- [ ] Patient enough to tolerate a rough edge in week one without it becoming a story told badly to five other people

**Visibility fit — nice to have, matters for what comes after client #1:**
- [ ] Reasonably well-connected or well-regarded in their trade or community — client #1's value is partly who they'll introduce you to next
- [ ] Would plausibly be comfortable being named as a reference or a short case study later, once there's something real to point to

A contact who clears **business fit + relationship fit** is worth a message. **Readiness** and **visibility** are what decide the *order* you approach people in, not whether you approach them at all.

### Who NOT to start with

- The loudest sceptic in the community, even if you're personally close — word-of-mouth cuts both ways, and a bad first impression from a vocal critic costs more than a slow start with someone else.
- Someone outside the actual ICP just because they're an easy yes (e.g. a retail shop with no real WhatsApp order volume) — a client who doesn't fit won't generate the pain-relief story that makes the next referral easy.
- Someone who'd need more hands-on support than you can actually give in week one — the concierge-onboarding offer in `PITCH_DECK.md` Slide 9 only works if you can genuinely deliver it.

## Message templates

Use your own natural voice and code-switch the way you actually would with that specific person — these are structure and content, not a script to copy verbatim. Where a greeting or phrase would naturally be in Urdu, Punjabi, or Hindi with a specific contact, write it the way you'd actually say it to them; that's not something to standardise into a template.

### 1. Initial outreach — WhatsApp text

Short. Personal. Doesn't try to close in the message — it asks for a conversation, nothing more.

> Oi [name], random one — you know how you're always saying [specific thing they've complained about, e.g. "you lose track of half your WhatsApp orders"]? I've actually built something for exactly that, been working on it a while, it's live now. Not trying to sell you anything over text — got 10 mins this week for me to show you? Genuinely think it'd help your lot specifically.

Keep the "specific thing" specific — a generic version of this message reads like a pitch, a specific one reads like a friend who remembered something.

### 2. Initial outreach — voice note version

WhatsApp voice notes are a completely normal register in this community and often land warmer than text. Rough spoken structure, don't write it out and read it verbatim:

> "Oi [name], kaisa hai — listen, quick one. So you know I've been building stuff on the side — I've actually built this thing that reads your WhatsApp orders, tells you which ones are real, writes the reply for you, does your quotes and invoices too. It's actually working, not just an idea. I want to show two or three people I trust before anyone else sees it, and you're one of them. You got a bit of time this week, I'll come to you, two minutes and you'll get it."

### 3. Once they say yes — confirming the meeting

Sets expectations, keeps it low-pressure, plants the "you'd be early" framing before you even arrive:

> Sound, [day/time] works well for me. Bring your phone — I'll show you live on a real message, not slides. Fair warning, you'd be one of the first people properly using this, so I'm not trying to sign you up on the spot — just want your honest take.

### 4. During/after the conversation — the discovery questions (don't skip this)

Naturally, not as a survey, work in something close to `SURVEY.md` Q7 and Q11 once they've seen the demo:

> "Be honest with me — if this could just send the reply itself for the routine stuff, without you seeing it first, how would that actually sit with you? And would you rather see every single message before it goes, or only the ones that actually need you?"

Their answer matters as much as whether they say yes to trying it. Note it down after the conversation, even a rough paraphrase — this is the exact validation `SURVEY.md` was built to get, and it's happening for free inside a sales conversation instead of a separate ask.

### 5. Post-demo close — asking for the trial

Only send this if you didn't already get a verbal yes in the room (Slide 9 of `PITCH_DECK.md` should usually close it live):

> Like I said — no pressure, but I'd love to get this running on your number this week. I'll set the whole thing up myself and sit with you through the first few days. Fourteen days free, no card, you can stop any time if it's not for you. What do you reckon?

### 6. After client #1 is live and happy — the referral ask

Not for first outreach, but write it now so it's ready the moment it's earned — this is step 2 of the First 5 Clients plan in `agents/gtm-lead.md`:

> This has been going well, right? Do me a favour — who else do you know running something similar who'd feel the same pain you did? Happy to have the same conversation with them that I had with you, no pressure on either side.

This mirrors `SURVEY.md` Q23 almost exactly — another place the discovery instinct and the sales instinct are the same instinct.
